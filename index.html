Module 1 • Radhika Dhingra

Data Ingestion, Schema Inspection & Sentinel Cleaning

Raw astronomical telemetry contains observational sentinels, non-standard missing tokens, and unphysical values. This pipeline purges corrupted indicators and coerces strict numerical data types.

The 99.99 Sentinel Correction Astronomical Distortion

In raw catalogs, comets with diffuse comae that lack clear optical disc boundaries are assigned a placeholder magnitude of 99.99 mag. Without sanitization, this artifact catastrophically skews dataset averages:

Mean H (mag)
27.004 mag (Raw) → 20.525 mag (Cleaned)
Std Deviation
21.829 mag → 1.636 mag
Maximum H
99.99 mag (Sentinel) → 26.78 mag (Realistic)
Missing Indicators
??, ##, n/a, NA → Unified np.nan

Sanitization Pipeline Execution

# Replace non-standard tokens and astronomical sentinel
df = df.replace(["??", "##", "n/a", "N/A"], np.nan)
df["H (mag)"] = pd.to_numeric(df["H (mag)"], errors="coerce")
df["H (mag)"] = df["H (mag)"].replace(99.99, np.nan)

# Enforce 64-bit floating point precision
numeric_cols = ["H (mag)", "MOID (au)", "q (au)", 
                "Q (au)", "period (yr)", "i (deg)"]
for col in numeric_cols:
    df[col] = pd.to_numeric(df[col], errors="coerce")

# ISO-8601 Temporal Timestamp Parsing
df["Discovery Date"] = pd.to_datetime(df["Discovery Date"])

Cleaned dataset contains exactly 451 rows and 10 columns with 0 duplicate records. Hyperbolic comets with parabolic trajectories exhibit unbounded periods and aphelia.

Module 2 • Kalpak Kamble

Descriptive Statistics & Orbital Feature Engineering

Univariate distribution parameters, dispersion metrics, and Keplerian orbital features derived from perihelion (q) and aphelion (Q) apsides.

Keplerian Orbital Eccentricity (e)

Quantifies the elongation of the orbit, derived from perihelion $q$ and aphelion $Q$:

e = (Q - q) / (Q + q)

Sample Range: 0.0054 (nearly circular) to 0.999994 (hyper-eccentric comets). Mean $e = 0.526$.

Orbital Span (Radial Extent Δr)

Linear radial distance swept by the object between closest and farthest solar distances:

Δr = Q - q = 2ae

Sample Range: 0.03 AU (tight Aten orbits) to 338,830.42 AU (Oort cloud comets).

Orbit Class Profiling, Planetary Defense & Correlation

Identifying extreme objects, screening potentially hazardous asteroids, testing orbital hypotheses, and examining the top 10 closest Earth-approaching bodies.

Dual-Criterion Scientific Definition of a Potentially Hazardous Asteroid (PHA)

NASA defines an asteroid as potentially hazardous only if both criteria are satisfied:
1. Minimum Orbit Intersection Distance: $\text{MOID} \le 0.05\text{ AU}$ (~19.5 Lunar Distances / 7.48 million km).
2. Absolute Magnitude: $H \le 22.0\text{ mag}$ (corresponding to an estimated physical diameter $\ge 140\text{ meters}$).
Objects like (2010 LV108) have an ultra-close MOID of $0.002\text{ AU}$, yet are flagged as Non-Hazardous (N) because $H = 22.36\text{ mag}$ (too small to cause regional devastation upon atmospheric entry).

Project Synthesis

Key Conclusions & Planetary Defense Insights

Summary of primary astronomical findings and data engineering takeaways from Group 15's investigation.

01

Criticality of Sentinel Purging

Astronomical placeholders like comet magnitude $H = 99.99$ mag and string tokens distort summary statistics by orders of magnitude. Replacing sentinels restored the mean magnitude from an artificial $27.004$ mag to a physically true $20.525$ mag.

02

Earth-Crossing Orbit Dominance

Over 82% of NEOWISE discoveries belong to the Apollo (47.5%), Amor (27.3%), and Aten (7.3%) classes. Aten and Apollo asteroids exhibit the lowest mean MOIDs ($0.069$ AU and $0.125$ AU) and account for all 10 of the closest Earth-approaching objects.

03

Dual-Criterion Threat Screening

While 15.6% of evaluated asteroids are Potentially Hazardous (led by Apollo asteroid (2010 MU112) at MOID = $0.0003$ AU), several ultra-close objects ($MOID \le 0.005$ AU) are non-hazardous due to small physical sizes ($H > 22.0$ mag).
